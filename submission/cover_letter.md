Please consider “Cheap Attention, Costly Commitment: Cross-Route Inference in a
Dynamic Matching Market” for publication in the *Journal of Economic Behavior &
Organization*.

The paper studies a behavioral-information friction in a two-sided digital
matching market. Cheap short-term approaches are generated by sender cutoff
decisions, but receivers may evaluate those counts using a likelihood that is
too diagnostic of long-term attainability. The paper identifies the exact
histories on which this error changes dynamic acceptance, then embeds acceptance
in a two-sided participation game. It proves equilibrium existence, gives a
global uniqueness condition and local participation multipliers, and derives a
fold condition under which the high-liquidity equilibrium disappears.

The manuscript is relevant to JEBO because its central question is how a
cognitive and informational characteristic changes the operation of a market,
and because it combines behavioral belief formation with mathematical market
analysis. The closest prior theory already shows that coarse reasoning can
produce overoptimistic search and market failure. Our distinct contribution is
the endogenous cross-route signal, the explicit likelihood error, exact
history-level welfare characterization, and the transparency-to-participation
equilibrium channel.
