# Closest-literature matrix

This memo fixes the novelty boundary used in the manuscript. It should be
updated immediately before submission because forthcoming work can change the
comparison set.

| Study | Result closest to this paper | What this paper must not claim | Defensible distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Antler & Bachi (2022), *Searching Forever After*, AEJ: Micro | Coarse strategic reasoning creates overoptimism, excessive search, perpetual singlehood, and possible market failure. | That overoptimism can delay matching or that search technology can cause market failure. | Attention is an endogenous count of cheap cross-route offers; the error is an explicit likelihood misspecification; distorted histories and losses are exact; transparency changes acceptance and two-sided participation. |
| Antler, Bird & Fershtman (forthcoming), *Learning in the Marriage Market*, AEJ: Micro | Partners learn match-specific compatibility through dating; a steady-state matching equilibrium exists. | That dating can produce learning, selectivity, or sorting. | The signal comes from one relationship route and is used to infer attainability in another; beliefs are misspecified rather than calibrated compatibility learning. |
| Lee & Niederle (2015), *Propose with a Rose?*, Experimental Economics | Scarce “rose” messages credibly signal preference and affect acceptance. | That platform messages can transmit information or alter acceptance. | Cheap approaches are statistical signals produced by sender cost cutoffs, not scarce separating messages. |
| Fong (2024), *Effects of Market Size and Competition in Two-Sided Markets*, Marketing Science | Information about opposite-side market size reduces participation and raises selectivity in a field experiment. | That perceived market size can causally change dating-platform participation or selectivity. | The paper supplies a route-specific inference mechanism, a two-sided fixed point, and slope/fold conditions that organize those effects. |
| Andrew & Adams (2025), *Revealed Beliefs and the Marriage Market Return to Education*, QJE | Dynamic hypothetical choices identify subjective beliefs about future marriage offers. | That beliefs about future marriage offers matter for dynamic choices or can be elicited structurally. | The object is the endogenous source of a likelihood error and its equilibrium participation feedback, not a belief-identification method. |
| Abebe et al. (2025), *Matching Frictions and Distorted Beliefs*, Economic Journal | A two-sided job-fair experiment finds distorted beliefs and changes in search strategies. | That matching frictions can sustain distorted beliefs on both sides. | The theory isolates cross-route statistical inference and derives exact acceptance, participation, and tipping results. |

## One-sentence contribution claim

The paper identifies when endogenous attention in a cheap relationship route is
misread as evidence about costly commitment, and shows how the resulting
acceptance error propagates through a two-sided participation game, including a
formally characterized liquidity fold.
